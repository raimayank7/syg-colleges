Central universities in India

Set up by an Act of Parliament and funded by the central government. The UGC website lists 57 as on 11 November 2026.

A central university is created by an Act of Parliament. The central government funds it, and the President of India is its Visitor. Admission to most undergraduate courses in central universities is through the Common University Entrance Test (CUET).

Because they are few and well funded, seats are in demand across the country. Fees are usually low compared with private universities.

Where they are

The 57 entries are spread over 31 of the 34 states and union territories in the UGC lists. The most are in Delhi (7), Uttar Pradesh (6), Bihar (4), Telangana (4), Andhra Pradesh (3). 20 states and union territories have only one each. 3 states and union territories have none on this list.

Using this list

The table is sorted by state, then by name. Type a town or part of a name in the box to narrow it. The "UGC status" column is explained under the table. If an institution tells you it is one of these and you cannot find it here, look for it on ugc.gov.in before you believe it; names that sound alike are common. To see every kind of university in one state together, go to universities by state.

What "UGC status" means

  • 2(f): the university is on the UGC's list under Section 2(f) of the UGC Act.
  • 12B: it has also been declared fit to receive central grants under Section 12B.
  • Section-III: it is a deemed to be university, declared under Section 3 of the Act.

These are the UGC's own labels, shown as on its website. They say that the university is recognised. They do not rate its quality.

Questions people ask

How many central universities are there in India?

The UGC website lists 57 as on 11 November 2026.

Which state has the most?

Delhi (7), then Uttar Pradesh (6), then Bihar (4), among the 31 states and union territories that have at least one.

Source and date. The lists are those published on the website of the University Grants Commission (ugc.gov.in), read on 11 November 2026. A university can be added, renamed or removed at any time.
